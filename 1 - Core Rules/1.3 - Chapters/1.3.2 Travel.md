Journey Chapters follow the party as they travel between locations. The world is vast, and the road between places is rarely safe or uneventful.

# Progress

Every Journey has a **Progress Track** that represents the party's advance toward their destination. The Guide sets the length of the track based on the nature of the journey:

- **Fair** (6 Progress) -- Known roads, civilized lands. The path is clear and the dangers are few.
- **Perilous** (8 Progress) -- Hostile terrain, uncertain paths, or lands where trouble finds you. Most journeys through the wild fall here.
- **Harrowing** (10 Progress) -- Desperate crossings through blighted wastes, enemy territory, or places the world has forgotten. There is no stopping until you're through.

The party marks Progress through **Navigation** and by resolving **Road Scenes**. When the track is full, the party arrives at their destination.

Long but straightforward trips are handled as multiple Fair journeys with Camps between them. A Harrowing journey is not about distance -- it's about endurance.

# Weather

At the start of a Journey, the Guide rolls for **Weather**. The result sets the conditions for the journey -- what the sky looks like, what the air feels like, and what it means for the party mechanically.

Weather tables vary by **region** and **season**. Each table is a d6 roll that gives a tonal description and a mechanical effect. Effects typically take one of the following forms:

- **Navigate penalty** -- Fog, storms, or extreme conditions may make the Navigate roll Difficult or Arduous.
- **Travel Action restrictions** -- Severe weather may make certain Travel Actions unavailable (e.g. Scouting in a blizzard, Foraging in a wasteland storm).
- **A boon** -- Rare perfect conditions may refresh a resource or grant a small benefit.
- **No effect** -- Ordinary weather. The world is what it is.

The Guide interprets the weather through the lens of the region and the fiction. A severe result in the mountains is an ice storm; in the Blight, it's a choking spore cloud; on the steppe, it's a wind that strips the skin raw.

If the fiction demands it, the Guide may roll for new weather mid-journey -- a sudden shift as the party crosses into new terrain or as conditions worsen.

# The Road

During a Journey, the Guide presents **Road Scenes** -- moments along the way that bring the world to life. Each Road Scene is tied to a **Domain** that reflects the terrain or the nature of what the party encounters.

When a Road Scene is resolved, the party marks **1 Progress** by default. Exceptions are noted in the scene types below and in Travel Actions.

## Vistas
A moment of atmosphere. The party crests a hill and sees the valley below, choked with pale fungus. A ruined aqueduct stretches across the gorge. The treeline ends and the moor begins, flat and featureless to the horizon.

Vistas don't require rolls. They set the tone, establish the Domain of the area, and give the party information about what lies ahead or around them. The party marks 1 Progress.

## Encounters
Something on the road demands a response. A washed-out ford. A stranger who won't move aside. A storm rolling in. A beast stalking the party from the treeline.

Encounters typically require one or two action rolls to resolve. They are not full adventures -- they are moments of tension that the party must navigate before moving on. The party marks 1 Progress, or 0 if the encounter goes badly and costs the party time.

## Revelations
Information that changes the picture. A body wearing the sigil of the faction you're heading toward. Smoke rising from where the village should be. A trail of something that shouldn't exist this far from the Blight.

Revelations recontextualise the destination or the journey itself. They might cause the party to change course, prepare differently, or arrive with new questions. The party marks 1 Progress. A Revelation that changes the party's destination may reset or adjust the Progress Track at the Guide's discretion.

# Travel Actions

At the start of a Journey, each player chooses a **Travel Action** -- something their character does while on the move. One player **must** choose to **Navigate**; the rest choose freely from the common list below or from a *Travel Action* on their playbook.

Travel Actions resolve at the start of the Journey, before the first Road Scene. The Guide may call for a new Navigate roll if the fiction demands it -- the path splits, a storm obscures the trail, or a Revelation changes the route.

## Navigate (Mandatory)
Keep the party on course. Make an action roll, typically using **Discern** and a relevant Domain.

On a **Strong Hit**, the party finds good footing. Mark 2 Progress.

On a **Weak Hit**, the party pushes forward. Mark 1 Progress.

On a **Failure**, the party makes no headway. Mark 0 Progress. The Guide introduces a complication -- an unplanned Road Scene, dangerous terrain, or the party is turned around.

## Scout
Range ahead and look for danger or opportunity. Make an action roll, typically using **Hunt** or **Discern**.

On a hit, the Guide reveals information about an upcoming Road Scene before the party walks into it -- the nature of a threat, a shortcut, or a place of interest off the path. On a **Strong Hit**, the next Road Scene marks +1 additional Progress.

## Forage
Gather food, water, materials, or useful items along the way.

Refresh 1 **Favour**.

## Keep Watch
Stay alert and guard the group against threats.

Refresh 1 **Fate**.

## Playbook Travel Actions
Some playbook abilities are marked with *Travel Action*. These replace a common Travel Action -- a character who uses their playbook's Travel Action does not also pick from the common list.
