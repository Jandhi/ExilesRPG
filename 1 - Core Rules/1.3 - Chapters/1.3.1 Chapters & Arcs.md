The game is divided into **Chapters** and **Arcs**.

A **Chapter** is a self-contained unit of play with a clear driving question. Sessions usually consist of one or two Chapters. Every Chapter ends with **Camp**.

An **Arc** is a long-term objective spanning multiple Chapters, often centred on a location, faction, or villain.

# The Session

Each session follows a rhythm: **Horizon**, then one or more **Chapters**, ending in **Camp**.

## Horizon

At the start of each Chapter, the party takes stock. Where are you? What do you know? What do you want?

The Horizon is a conversation -- between the players, and between the players and the Guide. It might be a quick exchange on the road ("There's smoke to the east -- do we investigate?") or an extended scene in a settlement (gathering rumours, resupplying, meeting contacts). Its length flexes to fit the fiction.

Sometimes a Horizon is rich enough to be a scene in its own right -- the party spends time in a port city, makes allies, gathers secrets, and trades trinkets before setting out. Other times it's a single moment of decision. The Guide controls the pacing of a session by how much breathing room they give the Horizon before the Chapter's engine kicks in.

The Horizon ends when the party has a direction and the Chapter type becomes clear.

## Chapter Types

### Journey
The party travels between locations. The Chapter is driven by a **destination** -- somewhere you need to reach. Along the way, the Guide presents Road Scenes: atmospheric vistas, encounters, and revelations tied to the Domains of the terrain. See [[1.3.2 Travel]] for details.

Some abilities are marked *Travel Action* -- these can only be used during Journey chapters.

A Journey ends when the party arrives at their destination.

### Venture
The party pursues a concrete objective in a specific place. Infiltrate the fortress. Clear the blighted well. Broker peace between two factions. Escort the refugee caravan through the pass.

A Venture has a clear goal and opposition. This is where the action system sees the most use -- rolls carry high stakes, and fallout is likely.

A Venture ends when the objective is achieved, abandoned, or catastrophically failed.

### Crisis
Something comes to the party uninvited. An ambush on the road. A plague sweeping through camp. A faction making its move. A betrayal from within.

The party didn't choose this -- the world forced it on them. A Crisis has urgency; the Horizon is compressed to a single moment of realisation, or skipped entirely.

A Crisis ends when the threat is resolved, survived, or escaped.

## Blurring Types

Chapter types are a starting posture, not a cage. A Journey can become a Crisis when bandits attack. A Venture can emerge from a Horizon spent gathering information in a settlement. The types help the Guide prepare and pace -- they are not rigid categories announced at the table.

## Camp

At the end of each Chapter, the party **Makes Camp**. This is described in [[1.3.3 Camp & Time]].

# Arcs

An Arc is a long-term objective spanning multiple Chapters. It often revolves around a specific location, faction, or villain.

Arcs provide the connective tissue between Chapters. While each Chapter has its own driving question, the Arc gives the party a reason to keep moving -- a destination on the distant horizon, an enemy whose plans are unfolding, a mystery whose pieces are scattered across the land.
