Trinkets and Secrets are resources that characters collect and spend during their adventures. Both are tied to a **Domain** reflecting how or where they were acquired. They are intentionally open-ended — their specific nature is defined in the fiction when you gain them, and their value is realised when you spend them.

# Trinkets

A Trinket is an exceptionally valuable or useful item —a cracked wand still warm to the touch, a stamp bearing a forgotten noble's seal, a vial of something that shouldn't exist.

You gain Trinkets through play: looting a ruin, receiving a gift, finding something unusual on the road. When you gain a Trinket, note it and its **Domain**.

**Spending a Trinket.** When a Trinket is fictionally relevant to what you're doing, you may spend it to gain an **Edge** on an action roll. The item is used up, traded away, given as a bribe, or otherwise leaves your possession. Describe how it helps —the cracked wand flares to life one last time, the noble's stamp convinces the guard you have authority, the vial buys the alchemist's cooperation.

# Secrets

A Secret is something your character knows that hasn't been spoken aloud yet —a hidden truth about a person, a place, a faction, or the world itself.

You gain Secrets through play: overhearing a conversation, reading a stolen letter, prying information out of an NPC, or through playbook abilities. When you gain a Secret, note it and its **Domain**.

**Spending a Secret.** When it's relevant to the situation, you may spend a Secret to **declare something true** about the fiction within the scope of its Domain. You are introducing a fact into the world —something your character knew, discovered, or pieced together.

The Domain of the Secret shapes what kind of truth you can declare:

- An **Order** secret might reveal that the lord has a hidden debt, or that two noble houses have a buried feud.
- A **Wild** secret might establish that there's a safe crossing upstream, or that the wolves in this forest answer to something.
- An **Outlaw** secret might declare that the merchant has a smuggling route, or that someone in the room is not who they claim to be.

The Guide does not reject a declaration outright, but they may **shape** the details or decide what consequences follow from the truth being known. The core fact stands —the Guide decides what it *means*.

Secrets can also be spent for an **Edge** on an action roll when the information gives you a direct advantage, just like a Trinket. Knowing the guard captain's weakness counts for something when you're trying to get past him.
