# Goals of Exiles RPG

## The Fantasy

Exiles RPG is a dark fantasy game about people who can never go home — and who they become because of it. The tone is **dark but hopeful**: the world is dangerous and full of mystery, but there is light if you fight for it. Think the fellowship and journey of *Lord of the Rings* with the cryptic, layered worldbuilding of *Dark Souls* — but brighter, warmer, more human.

Players should feel **curiosity and adventure** session to session, tempered by real struggle. The bonds between characters matter. This is a game about a fellowship on the road ahead, discovering what the world holds and who they're becoming along the way.

## The Characters

Characters are **wanderers** — strangers to the world by nature, not necessarily by a single dramatic event. Some were banished, some drifted, some simply never belonged. What matters is that they're outsiders: foreign to the places they pass through, learning as they go. This foreignness is a feature — it gives players permission to ask questions, discover things, and approach the world with genuine curiosity. The player, who is also new to this world, relates directly to their character.

The stranger identity **shifts over a campaign**. Early play is driven by curiosity — learning the world, its customs, its dangers. Later play is driven by agency — having enough understanding (and scars) to actually change things.

Characters are defined by **what they're becoming**, not where they came from. Transformation is the arc. The best sessions end with a player saying *"my character changed."*

Death is **earned and dramatic** — it comes through the fallout spiral, never as a surprise. When a character dies, it should feel like the culmination of a story, not a bad dice roll.

Relationships between PCs are a **fellowship**. You're in this together. Trust is assumed, and bonds deepen through shared hardship.

## The Play

Combat, exploration, and social play carry **equal weight**. None is the "real game" — the system supports all three without favouring one.

The world feels like a **road ahead**: there is direction and momentum, but detours and discovery are welcome. You're always moving forward.

Gear is **light and narrative**. A sword is a sword. What matters is who you are, what you know (Secrets), and what you carry (Trinkets) — not stat blocks on equipment.

## The Table

- **3–5 players** plus a Guide
- Works at **any campaign length** — from short arcs to long campaigns
- Guide prep target is **light** (a page of notes), with **near-zero** being possible — the system provides seeds, tables, and fiction-first tools so that cool stories emerge without heavy prep

## What Makes This Game Different

- **The Domain system.** Ten Domains are the lens for the entire world — not just magic, but knowledge, culture, danger, and identity. Everything connects back to them.
- **Emergent stories without prep.** The system is designed so that interesting fiction arises from play itself — from the fallout spiral, from Trinkets and Secrets, from the session structure. The Guide doesn't need to write a plot; the tools generate one.
- **Consequences that transform.** The stress and fallout system doesn't just hurt characters — it changes them. Fallout is where the most interesting character moments live.
- **Camp as ritual.** Sessions end at camp, where the party reflects, bonds, and grows. It's where the real story lands.

## What This Game Is Not

- Not a **tactical combat sim** — no grid, no feat trees, no build optimization (not Pathfinder)
- Not a **doom spiral** — the world is hard but not hopeless, and player agency is never undermined (not Call of Cthulhu)
- Not rules for every situation — the fiction and the Guide fill gaps, not a rulebook

## Design Principles

1. **Simple to play.** Players should be able to pick it up fast. No situation where play halts because someone doesn't know what to do.
2. **Simple to run.** Little to no balancing required. Pre-generated content and generative systems provide seeds to improvise from.
3. **Inspiring.** Thematic content that makes people want to create characters and tell stories. The Domains, playbooks, and fallout tables should spark imagination.
4. **Fiction-first.** Rules encourage roleplay, exploration of character drives, and improvisation. Abilities encourage players to spend rather than hoard, act rather than plan, and embrace interesting hardship.
5. **Collaborative worldbuilding.** The world belongs to the whole table, not just the Guide. Players introduce facts and details — the Guide shapes what they mean. 
6. **Modular.** Subsystems can be inserted or removed to fit the campaign and setting.
7. **A solid gameplay loop.** Horizon → Chapter → Camp creates variable pacing with natural moments of rest between action.
